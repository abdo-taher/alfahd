## Strategy Overview: Cost Content for Premium Contractors

The instinct of many premium contractors is to avoid publishing pricing content. This instinct is strategically wrong for SEO.

**Why cost content works for Al-Fahad:**

1. **Buyers who research costs are the most serious buyers.** A developer or project manager searching "aluminum facade cost Riyadh" has a real project budget. They are 3–4x more likely to convert than a visitor arriving via an informational query.

2. **Cost queries have almost no quality competition.** The current Saudi aluminum/glass/steel search landscape has no authoritative cost guide content. Al-Fahad can rank #1 for high-intent cost queries within 90 days.

3. **Cost content can be written to protect premium positioning.** The strategy is not to publish rate cards — it is to explain the *range of costs*, the *factors that drive cost*, and *why the cheapest contractor is often the most expensive decision*. This positions Al-Fahad as the expert while framing price competition correctly.

4. **Cost pages are the highest-converting non-transactional pages.** When a buyer calculates that their project is in Al-Fahad's service range, the next action is to request a quote. Cost pages funnel directly into the RFQ flow.

**The golden rule for cost content:** Give enough information to be genuinely useful (ranges, factors, examples) but always direct the buyer to request a custom quote for accurate pricing. This captures the lead without locking into a price commitment.

```
Commercial keyword search
    ↓

    ↓
```

## Brand Positioning in Cost Content

Cost pages must protect Al-Fahad's premium positioning. Key framing principles:

1. **Lead with range, not minimum.** Never anchor the buyer at the lowest price point. Present the full range and explain what determines where a project falls in that range.
2. **Explain the cost of cheap.** Each cost page includes a "Why the cheapest quote is often the most expensive decision" section — covering re-work costs, warranty failures, and project delays from unqualified contractors.
3. **Certify the price.** Reference Al-Fahad's ISO quality, 25+ years, and 300+ projects in the context of cost content. "Our pricing reflects certified materials, skilled labor, and a warranty-backed delivery."
4. **Never publish a rate card.** Exact rates are not published because every project is priced individually. Range tables are published to set buyer expectations and qualify leads.
